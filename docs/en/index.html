Four modules, one inventory

Everything hangs off the real gear. A fixture has a channel count, a power draw and an owner: the same record serves the patch, the load calculation and the dispatch note.

WRITTEN

Rentals

Who has what, since when, until when. Out, back, and what never came back.

The inventory does not move when equipment goes out. It says what you own; what is out is calculated. Decrementing quantities on the way out would be simpler and wrong: one forgotten rental would leave a stock figure nothing ever puts right.

A rental gets invoiced: Scenika exports lines for Ohmnia to import, rather than building a second invoicing module here.

WRITTEN

Power

The draw of a rig, spread across circuits and boards, from the wattage already entered in the inventory.

Hungriest first, then into the first circuit that takes it. The rule is simple on purpose: a technician must be able to redo it in their head on site, because it is by hand that they plug in. A unit hungrier than a whole circuit is refused and named, never forced in.

Never presented as electrical certainty. A 16 A circuit at 230 V holds about 3,600 W in theory and less in practice: the margin is shown. A mistake here kills a show, or worse.

The DMX calculator, free and whole

It does one calculation, properly, storing nothing and asking for nothing. It is not a crippled demo: a tool cut down on purpose annoys instead of converting.

Adresso

A fixture in 16-channel mode addressed at 001 occupies 001 to 016: the next one cannot start before 017. Overlapping addresses is the classic mistake, and it is the first thing the calculator catches.

A universe holds 512 channels. Past that you need a second one — the calculator says so and moves across, instead of producing an impossible patch.

The formula lives in one place, shared by the page and the application. An automated check refuses to let the page redefine a function of the module or rewrite the 512 limit: it is the only way to stop the two calculators drifting apart.

What we stand for

Scenika follows the ResonLab rules, the same ones as Ohmnia and Acustika.

01

No connection

No account, no subscription, no usage tracking. Your inventory never leaves your machine.

02

Together, if you choose it

A rental company has several people touching the same inventory. Nexika installs on your own machine — and local mode stays the default.

03

Messages that explain

Precise, never a raw technical error. When the software refuses a patch, it says which fixture overlaps which.

04

Nothing you cannot reopen

Readable formats, simple code, no closed format. In ten years your inventory will still open.

05

What is not guaranteed is said

The power calculation is an aid, not an electrical inspection. What is indicative is announced as such.

06

Checked, not claimed

The DMX calculation is covered by tests, including the three known traps. A failing check beats a lying document.
